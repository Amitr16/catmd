## Key translation notes

- **"고양이 집사" (cat butler/servant)** — Korean cat owners self-deprecatingly call themselves "집사" (servants of the cat). This is universally recognised cat-culture vocabulary in Korea. Using it in the title signals brand fluency in Korean cat culture.
- **"우리 고양이" (our cat) over "당신의 고양이" (your cat)** — Korean uses "우리" (our) where English uses "your" for things in your life (family, home, pet). "우리 고양이" reads more naturally than the literal "당신의 고양이."
- **해요체 register** — Polite but warm. "-입니다" / "-합니다" / "-요" endings throughout. Avoids the casual "-야/-아" (too informal for marketing) and the overly formal "-옵니다" (too stiff).
- **Loanwords are fine** — "AI", "보디랭귀지", "포스트카드", "트리아지", "체크인", "포스터", "타임랩스" — all katakana-style loanwords are normal in Korean tech-marketing register.
- **"냥이" (nyangi)** — A cute, playful Korean term for cat (from the meow sound "냐옹" + diminutive ending "이"). Used in alt-title option 4. Wouldn't use in formal copy, but for one playful headline it works.
