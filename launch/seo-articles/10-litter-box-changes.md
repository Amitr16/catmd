# Litter Box Changes: Frequency, Consistency, Blood, and What They Mean

The litter box is the single most underrated diagnostic tool in feline health. Changes in frequency, volume, consistency, or color almost always precede visible illness. Learning to read the signals can add years to your cat's life.

## What "normal" looks like

| Variable | Normal |
|---|---|
| Urination frequency | 2-4 times per day |
| Clump size | 2-3 tablespoons each, consistent |
| Stool frequency | 1-2 times per day |
| Stool consistency | Firm but not rock-hard, easy to scoop, no mucus |
| Stool color | Medium to dark brown |
| Urine color | Pale yellow to amber, consistent |
| Litter box use | In the box, consistently |

Variations from this baseline — especially sustained ones — deserve attention.

## Urinary changes

### Large, frequent clumps (polyuria)
Cat is peeing more volume than usual. Almost always paired with polydipsia (increased drinking). Common causes:
- **Chronic kidney disease** (diluted urine, lost concentrating ability)
- **Hyperthyroidism**
- **Diabetes mellitus**
- **Hypercalcemia**

Action: vet visit within a week for senior cats; sooner if other signs present.

### Small frequent clumps (pollakiuria)
Cat is making many short trips, possibly straining, possibly not producing much. Causes:
- **FIC (feline idiopathic cystitis)**
- **Bladder stones**
- **UTI** (less common than FIC in cats)
- **Urethral obstruction** (males — **emergency**)

Action: same-day vet, especially for males.

### No clumps at all (anuria)
Cat is not producing urine. Emergency — see [cat straining in the litter box](03-straining-litter-box.md).

### Pink, red, or orange urine
Blood in urine. Causes:
- **FIC** (most common)
- **Bladder stones**
- **UTI** (Chlamydophila, E. coli)
- **Urethral obstruction**
- **Trauma**
- **Kidney disease**
- **Cancer**

Action: vet visit within 24 hours. Bring a fresh urine sample if you can collect one.

### Strong-smelling urine
Normal cat urine has a distinctive smell. A sudden sharp or sweet smell can indicate:
- **UTI** (stronger ammonia)
- **Diabetes** (sometimes sweet/fruity from ketones)
- **Kidney disease**

Action: vet visit within a few days if sustained.

## Fecal changes

### Diarrhea
- Small-bowel diarrhea: large volume, few trips per day, sometimes weight loss
- Large-bowel diarrhea: small volume, frequent trips, mucus, sometimes fresh blood

Acute causes: dietary change, parasite, infection, toxin.
Chronic causes: IBD, lymphoma, food allergy, exocrine pancreatic insufficiency.

Action: 24 hours of mild diarrhea in an otherwise healthy cat can be monitored. Longer than that, or any diarrhea in a kitten/senior, warrants a vet call.

### Constipation
- Straining to defecate with no output or small hard pellets
- Often paired with vomiting once the colon is distended
- Mature intact males and senior cats are predisposed

Causes: dehydration (often secondary to CKD), megacolon, pelvic injury, hairballs, enlarged prostate.

Action: vet visit within 48 hours. Chronic constipation can progress to megacolon, which is much harder to treat.

### Black, tarry stool (melena)
Digested blood from upper GI bleeding. Causes:
- **Ulcer** (NSAIDs can cause — never give human NSAIDs to cats)
- **Pancreatitis**
- **Hemangiosarcoma**
- **Tumor**
- **Severe parasite burden**

Action: same-day vet.

### Fresh red blood on stool
Lower GI bleeding. Usually less dangerous than melena but still a call.

### Mucus on stool
Inflammation of the colon. Short-term: dietary. Long-term: IBD.

### Pale or white stool
Indicates bile isn't reaching the intestines. Liver or biliary disease. Vet visit within 48 hours.

## Behavioral changes

### Going outside the box
First question: medical or behavioral?

- **Medical causes:** FIC, UTI, arthritis (painful to step into high-sided box), litter aversion from a previous painful experience.
- **Behavioral causes:** litter type preference, box cleanliness, location stress, number of cats vs. boxes (rule: N cats + 1 box), new pet/person.

Start with a vet visit to rule out medical. It's very often medical.

### Increased vocalizing while in the box
Urinary pain. Treat as FIC or obstruction until proven otherwise — especially in males.

### Longer time in the box
Straining. See vocalizing.

### Avoiding the box entirely
The box has become associated with pain (they had a painful urination there once). Even after the medical issue is resolved, they may need a clean box in a new location to reset the association.

## The N+1 rule

If you have N cats, you need N+1 litter boxes, in different locations. One cat can "block" another from a single box (politely, by lingering near it). Inadequate box count causes behavioral issues.

## How to read a clean box

Clean the box once daily. Look for:
- **Clump size consistency** — suddenly smaller or larger is a signal
- **Number of clumps per day** — trend over a week
- **Hardness of stool** (normal stool holds its shape but breaks cleanly when scooped)
- **Color changes** (suddenly darker, lighter, black, pale)
- **Strings of mucus**

## Red flags — same-day vet

- Blood in urine
- Straining with no urine output (male especially)
- Black tarry stool
- Diarrhea with blood + hiding or lethargy
- No urination in 12+ hours
- No defecation in 3+ days with straining
- Dramatic increase in urine volume in a senior cat
