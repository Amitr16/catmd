## Primary keyword targets (ordered by priority)

### Tier A — direct-intent, low competition

These have buyer intent ("I'm looking for this exact thing") and limited competition. Target in app title / short description.

| Keyword | Est. monthly volume | Competition | Why target |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cat symptom checker` | 1K-5K | Low | Direct triage intent; no cat-only competitor ranks well |
| `AI vet for cats` | 500-2K | Very low | Novel category; own the phrase |
| `cat health app` | 5K-15K | Medium | Broader but high-intent; our feature set matches |
| `cat triage` | 100-500 | Very low | Professional-adjacent term; we dominate |
| `feline AI` | 100-500 | Very low | Differentiator phrase |
| `feline health tracker` | 500-2K | Low | Matches our dashboards |
| `cat medical app` | 500-2K | Low | Direct intent |

### Tier B — broad-intent, high competition

Higher volume but need to earn with strong content/reviews over time.

| Keyword | Est. volume | Competition | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pet symptom checker` | 10K-50K | High | Secondary; we position as "cat-only" differentiator |
| `pet health app` | 20K+ | Very high | Don't target directly; use in long description |
| `vet app` | 15K-40K | High | Long-tail pivot: "AI vet triage for cats" |
| `cat care` | 10K+ | Very high | Long description only |
| `pet AI` | 2K-10K | Medium | Long description |

### Tier C — specific-condition keywords (SEO gold for organic)

These drive traffic to catmd.pet/library articles, not directly to the app store, but they're high-intent. See SEO article plan.

- `why is my cat vomiting`
- `cat straining to urinate` (urethral obstruction — literally our strongest differentiator)
- `cat not eating for 24 hours`
- `cat hiding and lethargic`
- `cat kidney disease symptoms`
- `feline grimace scale`
- `cat breathing fast while sleeping`
- `why is my cat losing weight`

## Competitor listing analysis (3 direct competitors)

### 1. DogMD (dogmd.app)
- **Title:** `DogMD: AI Vet for Dogs` (22 chars)
- **Short desc:** emphasizes "AI vet" + "symptoms"
- **Strengths:** owns the "AI vet for [species]" pattern; clean brand
- **Weakness:** explicitly excludes cats — our core opening
- **Takeaway:** mirror their title convention (we're already doing this). Their success proves users search "AI vet for [species]".

### 2. TTcare (t-tcare.com)
- **Title:** varies by region, emphasizes "Pet Symptom AI"
- **Short desc:** pet-general (dogs + cats + more)
- **Strengths:** more features, bigger team, established
- **Weakness:** dilutes cat advice; cat scans sometimes produce dog-coded results
- **Takeaway:** our "cat-only" positioning is the wedge. Our long description should contrast with "generic pet apps."

### 3. Tably (tably.ca)
- **Title:** `Tably - Cat Pain Detector`
- **Short desc:** emphasizes Feline Grimace Scale
- **Strengths:** single-feature focus, vet-endorsed
- **Weakness:** $5/mo for one feature; narrow utility
- **Takeaway:** include Feline Grimace Scale as first-tier feature in our description (we already do). Our pain score + full triage + dashboards is strictly more valuable for the same price.

### 4. Generic pet-AI apps (Buoy for Pets, Petriage, VetAI, etc.)
- Mostly dog-centric
- Most pivoted to B2B (vet-clinic-facing) after consumer didn't scale
- Our consumer moat: cat-only + honest-AI positioning + retention-focused features

## Review-velocity strategy (first 30 days post-launch)

Google ranks apps by **review velocity**, not just star count. Get reviews fast.

**Target:** 30+ reviews in first 30 days. Average ≥4.5.

**How:**
1. In-app prompt — ask for review after 3rd successful scan (native `requestReview` API)
2. Email ask to beta testers after launch — "if CatMD helped, would love 30 seconds of your time"
3. **Do not** ask at install, at paywall, or after error screens — always after a positive moment
