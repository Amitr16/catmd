**Pitch window:** Weeks 6-7
**Realistic response rate:** ~3-5%. Most won't reply. The 1-2 that do can move 10x more traffic than all of Tier 1+2 combined, so this is a numbers game with a fat right tail.
**Embargo policy:** **The Verge, Wired, NYT will often want exclusivity** for a real feature. Engadget, Fast Company, Vice are usually fine with parallel coverage. If you want a Verge or Wired feature, you offer them first-look + 24-48 hour exclusive — and you don't pitch the rest until they pass.
**Lead time:** 2-4 weeks pitch-to-publish at minimum. **Tier 3 does not move fast.** Do not pitch Tier 3 expecting Week-7 coverage — pitch in Week 6-7 expecting Week 9-10 coverage at earliest.

  1. **Why now** — a news hook (public Play Store launch, AI-app boom, indie-dev-vs-big-tech narrative)
  2. **A data point** — closed-test retention number, beta user count, install velocity, anything specific

# CROSS-TIER OPERATING NOTES

### Email-pattern verification
Before you send any of the inferred-pattern emails above, run each name through:
1. **Muck Rack** (muckrack.com) — most outlets have free outlet pages with verified journalist contacts
2. **RocketReach** (rocketreach.co) — paid but $40 buys you 50 verified emails, a one-shot expense for a Tier-3 pitch round
3. **The outlet's own staff page** — Engadget, The Verge, and Fast Company all publish bylines with click-throughs to author pages that often contain contact info

### Pitch hygiene checklist (Founder, before hitting send)
- [ ] Editor name spelled correctly and confirmed in role within last 90 days
- [ ] First sentence references something they wrote in the last 60 days (Tier 3 only — non-negotiable)
- [ ] Subject line is under 70 chars, no emojis, no "[PITCH]" prefix
- [ ] Press kit link works (Drive folder with screenshots, demo video, founder photo, one-pager)
- [ ] Play Store listing is live and the link in the pitch goes to it
- [ ] You can answer "why now" in one sentence
- [ ] No follow-up sooner than 5 business days. One follow-up max.

### What to NOT do
- **Don't send the same pitch to two reporters at the same outlet.** They share inboxes and notice. Pick one.
- **Don't pitch Verge / Wired and Tier 2 in the same week.** If Verge bites, Tier 2 won't matter for 30 days.
- **Don't lead with "AI for cats" in the subject.** Every editor in 2026 has filtered "AI for X" pitches into a folder they don't read. Lead with what's specific (Feline Five, vet-ready PDF, body-language read).
- **Don't fabricate a name to make a pitch feel personal.** If the line above says "(NO NAMED LEAD)", use the tipline. A wrong name burns the whole outlet for 6 months.

### Founder-arc story (use across all three tiers — adjust emphasis)
- Solo dev, no co-founder, no VC
- $500/mo total marketing budget
- Android-first contrarian bet (everyone else chases iOS Q3)
- Built on Feline Five — the only peer-reviewed cat personality framework — because saccharine pet AI is wrong about cats
- The cat replies are *judgmental*, and that's the design choice that makes the diary unfakeable
